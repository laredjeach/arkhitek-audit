# Niche Lens: Dental Practice

## Business Context

Dental practices are high-LTV local businesses. A new patient who stays with the practice is worth $1,500–$5,000+ over their lifetime. Implants, Invisalign, and cosmetic cases can be $3,000–$8,000+ per patient. Practices compete hard on Google Maps and local search.

Decision-makers are typically the practice owner-dentist, practice manager, or office manager. They care about:
- New patient phone calls and appointment bookings
- Their Google review count and star rating
- Ranking for "dentist near me," "[city] dentist," and specific procedures (Invisalign, implants)
- Not looking outdated compared to corporate dental chains

Their typical website situation:
- Built by a dental-industry web company (Dental Intelligence, Solutionreach, ProSites, Smile Marketing)
- These platforms produce cookie-cutter sites with templated content, thin service pages, and almost no differentiation
- Or an older Wix/Squarespace site the dentist built themselves
- Schema coverage is usually partial — LocalBusiness or MedicalBusiness present but no AggregateRating, no FAQ schema, no procedure-specific schema

## Why Their Website Is Usually Weak

Dental practices pay a monthly fee to a "dental marketing" company that provides a template site, a few blog posts per year, and reputation management. The dentist has no idea what's on the site or how it ranks. They're told "your website is set up" and move on.

The result: dozens of dental sites in any metro area look nearly identical. The practices that stand out are the ones with a human voice, real photos, specific procedure content, and strong local schema.

## ROI Framing

- One new Invisalign patient: $4,000–$7,000 treatment
- One new implant patient: $3,000–$6,000 per implant
- Monthly hygiene patient: $150–$400/visit × 2 visits/year = $300–$800/year LTV
- Improving local pack ranking from #5 to #2 can double monthly new-patient calls
- "If better local schema visibility gets you two more Invisalign consults per month, that's $8,000–$14,000 in potential monthly revenue from one fix."

## Common Audit Findings for This Niche

1. **Generic, template-produced homepage** — "Welcome to [Practice Name]! We offer comprehensive dental care." No differentiation.
2. **No AggregateRating schema** despite 50–200 Google reviews — missing star-rating display in SERPs
3. **MedicalBusiness or Dentist schema missing** — LocalBusiness only, not the correct type
4. **Thin service pages** — one paragraph per service, no FAQ, no schema, nothing for Google to rank
5. **No llms.txt** — zero dental practices have this
6. **Booking CTA unclear** — "Contact us" instead of "Book a New Patient Appointment"
7. **Old platform with no customization** — ProSites or Smile Marketing template unchanged for 3+ years
8. **Multiple review platforms embedded** — Yelp, Google, Zocdoc widgets competing for space

## Outreach Tone Guidance

- Dentists are service-oriented, patient-focused, and often not technical. Speak to them in patient and practice terms, not SEO jargon.
- Avoid: "your site lacks structured data," "your meta descriptions are suboptimal."
- Use: "when someone searches 'dentist in [city],' here's what they see for your listing," "new patients Googling for Invisalign won't find you before [Competitor]."
- Practice managers respond to: time savings, new patient numbers, not embarrassing the dentist.
- Owner-dentists respond to: specific dollar impact, new patient flow, reputation signals.
- Keep it brief and warm. Dentists are busy. One specific finding is enough to get a reply.

## Closing Language That Works

- "Happy to send the short audit if it would be useful."
- "Would a 15-minute call to walk through what I found make sense?"
- "No sales pitch — just the findings. If any of it looks worth addressing, we can talk about what that would look like."

Avoid:
- "We specialize in dental marketing..."
- "Our dental SEO packages..."
- "Let's schedule a discovery call..."

## Sample Subject Lines

1. One thing I noticed on [Practice Name]'s Google listing
2. Quick note about [Practice Name]'s website
3. [City] dentist search — what's visible for [Practice Name]
4. Found a gap on [Practice Name].com worth sharing
5. Your Invisalign page — one improvement that might help
