# Niche Lens: Personal Injury Lawyer

## Business Context

Personal injury attorneys are high-intent, high-value local service businesses. A single signed client is worth $5,000–$50,000+ in contingency fees. Competition is intense in most metro markets — firms spend heavily on billboards, radio, and Google Ads — but website quality frequently lags.

The decision-maker is almost always the named partner or managing attorney. They care about:
- Signed cases, not traffic or impressions
- Their reputation — Google reviews and state bar standing matter
- Not embarrassing themselves in front of potential clients
- Ranking for "personal injury lawyer [city]" and "[practice area] attorney near me"

Their typical website pain points:
- Built by a legal directory company (Martindale, Avvo, FindLaw) with zero customization
- Or built on Elementor/Divi by a cheap freelancer who knew WordPress but not SEO
- Schema is almost always missing or copied from a template without review
- No AI search readiness — no llms.txt, no FAQ schema, no clear entity signals
- City pages either don't exist or are thin duplicates with city name swapped in
- Attorney bio pages are often the most poorly optimized pages on the site

## Why Their Website Is Usually Weak

Personal injury firms are busy with cases. Website maintenance falls to a paralegal or receptionist who has no digital marketing background. The site often went live 5–7 years ago and has not been touched since except for adding new blog posts.

Many firms pay a monthly retainer to a "legal SEO" company that provides mediocre work — generic blog content, thin city pages, no schema — but the client never sees the results because rankings are hard to measure.

## ROI Framing

Frame everything in cases, not traffic:
- One signed case: $5,000–$50,000 contingency (depending on practice and settlement)
- A typical PI firm closes 2–5% of web leads that reach the intake form
- Improving local search visibility by capturing one more keyword cluster can mean 10–20 more inquiries per month
- "If fixing your contact page meta description gets you one more phone call that turns into a signed case, that's [X]."

## Common Audit Findings for This Niche

1. **Title tag typos or generic firm-name-only titles** — attorney name with no practice area or location
2. **No LocalBusiness or LegalService schema** on homepage or practice-area pages
3. **Multiple H1s** — page builders (Elementor, Beaver Builder) frequently produce duplicate H1s
4. **Missing llms.txt** — zero firms have this; it's a strong differentiator talking point
5. **AggregateRating schema absent** despite dozens of Google reviews — losing star-rating display in SERPs
6. **City/practice-area pages that are thin** — under 300 words with no structured data
7. **Contact page 404 or outdated URL** — old /contact-us/ not redirected to /contact/
8. **Multiple review widgets** slowing the site and sending conflicting trust signals

## Outreach Tone Guidance

- Be direct but not aggressive. Attorneys are busy and will immediately screen out anything that sounds like spam.
- Reference a specific, visible, slightly embarrassing finding — not "your SEO needs work."
- Avoid terms like "optimize," "SEO strategy," "digital marketing services."
- Do not lead with price or packages. Lead with the finding, then the business impact, then an offer to share the full audit.
- Attorneys respond to: credibility, specificity, and ROI in dollars or cases.
- If you found a typo or broken element that's visible in Google search results, lead with that. Nothing works better.
- Keep the email under 100 words. Attorneys skim.

## Closing Language That Works

- "Happy to send the short audit if useful."
- "If it's helpful I can walk through the two or three things I'd fix first on a 15-minute call."
- "No pitch — just the findings. If any of it resonates, we can talk about next steps."

Avoid:
- "We'd love to partner with your firm..."
- "Our proven SEO system..."
- "Schedule a free consultation..."

## Sample Subject Lines

1. Quick note on your Google search listing
2. Found something on lawfirm.com worth 5 minutes
3. Your attorney page title — quick fix available
4. One thing I noticed on [Business Name]'s site
5. [City] personal injury search: what's holding [Business Name] back
