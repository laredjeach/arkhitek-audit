# Niche Lens: General (Local Service Business)

## Business Context

This lens applies to any owner-operated local service business: accountants, HVAC companies, roofers, plumbers, chiropractors, real estate agents, photographers, consultants, therapists, hair salons, and others.

The common thread:
- One or two decision-makers who are also the primary service providers
- Website is usually an afterthought, built once and rarely updated
- They generate most of their business through referrals and word of mouth — which means they've never had to think hard about their website, until now
- They know they "should" have a better website but haven't prioritized it

Key goals:
- Appear credible when someone Googles them after a referral
- Show up for local searches their competitors are capturing
- Make it easy for interested prospects to contact or book them

## Why Their Website Is Usually Weak

The website was built by a friend, a cheap freelancer, or a template platform (Wix, Squarespace, Weebly). It launched, it "works," and it hasn't been touched since. The owner doesn't think about it because they're busy running the business.

The result: outdated content, no schema, no local search signals, no AI visibility, and a contact form that may or may not work.

Common patterns:
- Homepage title is just the business name with no location or service ("Smith & Associates" vs. "Smith & Associates — Accountants in Thousand Oaks, CA")
- No Google Business Profile integration or schema tying the site to local search
- Phone number buried in the footer, not in the header or above the fold
- No social proof visible on the homepage (reviews, client logos, years in business)
- Mobile experience is broken or cramped

## ROI Framing

Tailor to the specific business, but general principles:
- For service businesses with any ticket above $500: one new client per month from better web visibility pays for most website improvements within the first quarter
- For recurring service businesses (accountants, cleaners, HVAC maintenance): LTV per client is 3–5× the first-year revenue
- "Better local search visibility means the people already looking for what you do can find you — instead of finding your competitor."

## Common Audit Findings for This Niche

1. **Homepage title is business-name-only** — no location, no service category
2. **No Organization or LocalBusiness schema** — Google can't confirm what the business is or where
3. **Missing NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency** — phone or address not visible on homepage
4. **No Google review count or star schema** — reviews exist but don't show in search results
5. **Contact form or CTA buried** — visitors have to scroll or click twice to find how to reach the business
6. **No llms.txt** — AI search tools can't accurately describe the business
7. **Thin or missing About page** — no story, no credentials, no trust signals
8. **Mobile-unfriendly design** — a majority of local searches happen on phones

## Outreach Tone Guidance

- Keep it simple and non-technical. The decision-maker is a busy small business owner, not a marketer.
- Avoid all jargon: schema, canonical, meta tags, structured data. Translate everything into plain English.
- Frame everything as "people searching for [service] in [city]" not "your search rankings."
- One specific finding. One plain-English impact. One offer. That's the whole email.
- Tone: direct, respectful, brief. Like a note from a neighbor who happens to know about websites.

## Closing Language That Works

- "Happy to send the short report."
- "No pressure — just thought it was worth flagging."
- "If it's useful, we could walk through it on a quick call."

Avoid:
- "We help businesses like yours..."
- "Our proven process..."
- "Let's hop on a discovery call..."

## Sample Subject Lines

1. Quick note about [Business Name]'s website
2. Found something on [Business Name].com worth knowing
3. One thing holding [Business Name] back on Google
4. [City] [service] search — what I found for [Business Name]
5. Short website note for [Business Name]
